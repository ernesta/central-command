Small, medium and large, as macOS draws them: no buttons beyond what a widget can do (tap opens the app, or the timer's Stop through an App Intent in a later step). They show what the Home page shows, in less.

2 · How the data reaches the widget
Built. With the widget switched on in Settings, the app writes data/widget/snapshot.json once a minute when something a widget shows has changed: today's tasks (at most five), the timer, the next meeting, the week's hours, the number of new Watch matches, the weather. Plain, versioned JSON; driven in the built app.
Not built. The widget itself is a small Swift app extension (WidgetKit) that reads that file and draws it. It cannot be made by the Electron build alone: it needs Xcode, a signing identity, and a shared folder (an App Group) the widget is allowed to read.
Your decision: whether to take that step, and whether the app should then be signed with your Apple developer account (the installed app is unsigned today).

    "date": "2026-10-15", "time": "14:00" },
  "week": { "minutes": 750, "aimMinutes": 2250 },
  "watchNew": 2,

Task titles are in this file, on your Mac, in your data folder. Nothing in it leaves the machine.
